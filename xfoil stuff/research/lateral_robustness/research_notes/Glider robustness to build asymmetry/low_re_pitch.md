# Low-Re thin flat-plate aerodynamics (Re 5k-60k) and persistent pitch swoops in small gliders

Context assumed throughout: 400 mm span, ~10 g, V_trim ~5.5 m/s, chord ~46 mm (Re ~17,000), trim CL 0.3-0.4, flat foamboard (5 mm, so t/c ~11 %, blunt LE) or 0.2-0.4 mm printed sheet (t/c ~0.4-0.9 %). Existing project notes this builds on: `research/plate_data/README.md` (Ananda 2012 AR 3 flat-plate tables, Re 60k-120k, with our own derived slopes) and `research/tumble_model/README.md` (Li et al. 2022 quasi-steady model, implemented and checked). Those numbers are not repeated except where needed for comparison.

Evidence tags: **[M]** measured / peer-reviewed experiment; **[Mod]** model, theory or simulation; **[H]** hobbyist practice; **[S]** speculation / my inference. Downloaded copies (untrusted data, nothing executed) are in `research/lateral_robustness/sources/` (`uhlig2013/`, `uma_flatplate/`, `akita_okamoto/`).

## 1. Measured lift, drag and moment for thin flat / slightly cambered plates at Re 5k-60k

### Takeaway
The only tabulated thin-flat-plate wing numbers I could actually read at Re ~10^4 are Okamoto & Jinba (2009): AR 4-6 rectangular 0.5 mm plates at Re 1.2-1.5x10^4 give lift slope 3.8-4.5 /rad (0.066-0.078 /deg) over -3..3 deg, a slope break (not a lift drop) at ~8 deg, CLmax 0.8-0.96 only at 33-40 deg, and (L/D)max only ~5.8-5.9 at ~4-5 deg. That L/D is well below the ~8 of the Ananda Re-60k plate the project's phugoid model used. Thin plates show no hysteresis in steady tunnel tests at Re ≥ 60k. Re-10k-30k hysteresis data for plates, and the Torres & Mueller / Sunada 2002 / Pelletier & Mueller tables themselves, were not reachable.

### Inferences
- **[S] The project's phugoid model likely overestimated L/D.** At Re ~1.3x10^4, thin AR 4-8 plates give (L/D)max ≈ 5.5-6.5 (Okamoto & Jinba), against ~8 for the Ananda Re-60k plate. Lanchester damping ζ ≈ 1/(√2·L/D) would then be ~0.11-0.13 rather than 0.09. That makes oscillations die out faster, not slower, so the low-Re drag penalty alone cannot explain swoops that persist.
- **[S] At trim the plate is in its linear range, but not far from the slope break.** With CL_a ≈ 0.066-0.078 /deg (AR 4-6 rectangles, Re 1.2-1.4e4), trim CL 0.3-0.4 needs alpha ≈ 4-6 deg. The slope break at ~8 deg then sits only ~2-4 deg (ΔCL ≈ 0.15-0.25) above trim. A phugoid or gust that raises alpha by a few degrees at the slow top of a swoop reaches the "stall" (slope break plus aft CP shift) that Kai sees.
- **[S] Foamboard is not a thin plate.** 5 mm on a ~46 mm chord is t/c ≈ 11 % with a blunt square LE, far outside every dataset above (t/c 1-4 %). Okamoto & Jinba explicitly see t/c affecting CDmin and L/D even within 1-1.7 %. Expect higher CD0, a different LE-separation onset and lower L/D for foamboard than for the 0.2-0.4 mm printed sheet. A sanded or chamfered LE plausibly matters (LE sharpness changed separation from ~8 deg in Okamoto 1996). This is untested here.
- **[S] Lift-slope evidence at Re ~2x10^4 conflicts.** Okamoto & Jinba (thin aluminium, Re 1.2-1.5e4) find slopes near or above lifting-surface theory. Uhlig & Selig (membrane, Re 25k) and Spedding & McArthur (Re 20-30k) find ~67 % of theory. The Málaga correlation extrapolated below its 40k floor predicts ~0.044 /deg for AR 6 at Re 17k (my arithmetic, eq. 9). That is a ~22 % drop from its own 60k value and far below Okamoto & Jinba's measured 0.078 /deg. Do not extrapolate the correlation; treat CL_a at Re 17k as uncertain by roughly ±30 %.
- **[S] The aerodynamic centre may be Re-dependent.** Ananda's dCM/dCL roughly doubles from 120k to 60k (0.019 to 0.042), so the AC moves forward as Re falls. If that trend continues to Re 1-2x10^4, slowing down at the top of a swoop moves the AC forward, a nose-up "speed-stability" term (Cm_u-like). That would push alpha up exactly when speed is lowest. This is an extrapolation and is not measured below 60k.

### Gaps
- Could not read the tables of Pelletier & Mueller 2000, Torres & Mueller 2004, Sunada 2002 / 1997, Okamoto & Azuma 2011, Mueller 1999 or Kunz & Kroo (paywalled; DTIC returned 403). No Re 10-30k numbers from them are given here.
- No measured static stall hysteresis loop for a thin flat plate at Re 10k-30k was found. "No hysteresis" is established only at Re ≥ 60k (Pelletier & Mueller, Ananda).
- No sweep or taper data for AR 3-6 plates at Re < 40k were found. Ananda has taper at 60k+ (in project CSVs); Okamoto & Jinba have only ellipse/rectangle/triangle planforms.
- Wang & Ristroph falling-plate work was covered only through Li et al. 2022 (already in the project). No separate Wang data were pulled.

## 2. Why small gliders keep swooping (phugoid vs stall porpoise) and what damps it

### Takeaway
Linear phugoid theory cannot explain swoops that last more than 10 cycles. With ζ ≈ 0.09-0.13, amplitude falls to ~0.3 % or less after 10 cycles. A persistent, slowly shrinking oscillation with small stalls at each crest means one of two things. Either the motion is (a) a nonlinear stall-limited cycle, i.e. Li et al.'s measured "bounding" mode (gliding bouts interrupted by soft stalls, which they liken to a Hopf bifurcation off steady gliding as the CoM moves aft), or (b) a lightly damped mode continually re-excited by air motion. The passive cures supported by evidence all move the glider away from the bounding boundary: CG forward (bigger static margin, with the decalage it requires), and trimming to a lower CL / farther from the slope break.

### Cited Findings
**Phugoid basics**
- Lanchester phugoid: ω_n ≈ √2·g/V, with CL and CD assumed constant. Drag is the only damping term, and with zero drag there is no damping. — [MIT 16.01 phugoid notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-01-unified-engineering-i-ii-iii-iv-fall-2005-spring-2006/4e932d24f9d4b17918820220ce1bd11e_phugoid.pdf); [numerical-MOOC phugoid lesson](https://nbviewer.jupyter.org/github/numerical-mooc/numerical-mooc/blob/master/lessons/01_phugoid/01_03_PhugoidFullModel.ipynb) [Mod]
  - Damping-ratio form: my standard-textbook form is ζ = 1/(√2·L/D), i.e. ζ = CD/(√2·CL). A search summary of the MIT notes rendered it as 1/√(2·L/D). I did not open the PDF to settle this, so it is unresolved here; the brief's form is the commonly quoted one. [Mod; check against Etkin/Nelson]
- In the classical phugoid alpha stays roughly constant, and the stalling alpha is never exceeded. — [Phugoid (Wikipedia mirror)](https://wikipedia2007.classicistranieri.com/p/h/u/Phugoid.html) [Mod, low-grade source]
- Large-amplitude glider phase portraits (Lanchester-type model with drag): small-R glides are a stable spiral sink. Larger initial conditions loop several times before settling, and a separatrix trajectory "stalls out" (V → 0) after k loops. — [Stony Brook course notes](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~scott/Book331/Qualitative_Classification_.html) [Mod]
- Nonlinear phugoid (Campos & Aguiar): starting below the lower steady speed diverges quickly toward stall; starting above it converges. The analysis assumes no stall. — [Aeronautical Journal](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/aeronautical-journal/article/on-the-inverse-phugoid-problem-as-an-instance-of-nonlinear-stability-in-pitch/B0713637699E5D95EC9E56D9E5F1AB5D) [Mod]
- Glider longitudinal dynamics split into slow (Lanchester phugoid) and fast timescales; steady glides proven stable by a Lyapunov function. — [Bhatta & Leonard](https://naomi.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/744/2021/03/BhattaLeonardFinalVersion.pdf) [Mod]

**Bounding / soft-stall limit cycle in thin-plate gliders (closest direct evidence)**
- Li, Goodwill, Wang & Ristroph 2022 (JFM 937 A6, Re ~10^3-10^4, acrylic plates in water plus paper planes in air) found the modes run in order as the CoM moves toward the LE: flutter, then progressive flutter, then **bounding** ("bouts of forward gliding interrupted by soft stalls"), then steady gliding, then diving. — [doi 10.1017/jfm.2022.89](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2022.89) [M]
  - Paper plane: bounding at l_CM/l = 0.14, gliding at 0.24, diving at 0.31. Steady gliding is attained for a balance-point location l_p/l in (0.22, 0.29). — same [M]
  - The bounding, flutter and progressive-flutter postures are statically stable attractors at larger alpha. They "are apparently dynamically unstable in free flight and give rise to oscillatory motions. The transition from gliding to bounding is reminiscent of a Hopf bifurcation in which a stable fixed point gives way to limit cycle oscillations." — same [M + authors' interpretation]
  - In bounding the plate "spends appreciable time at attack angles α ≈ 10-20° that are between the laminar and stalled flow regimes", where unsteady effects are expected. Their quasi-steady model gives bounding bouts shorter in period, with smaller alpha pulses, than experiment. Its bounding/gliding boundary sits at a somewhat larger CoE location than measured, i.e. the model under-predicts how far forward the CoM must go to kill bounding. — same [M vs Mod]
  - Gliding "has a long transient before reaching steady state" (also reproduced in this project's Step A runs: 5 of 24 starts at 0.24 still unsettled at 15 s). — same, and `research/tumble_model/README.md` [M/Mod]

**Unsteady / dynamic-stall hysteresis**
- In free flight at Re 25k, lift is larger on pitch-up than pitch-down even at |k| ≈ 0.015. A k-based effective-camber model explains only part of it; the authors cite additional contributions (vortex shedding, apparent mass, wing flex, laminar separation bubble). — [Uhlig & Selig 2013](https://m-selig.web.engr.illinois.edu/pubs/UhligSelig-2013-JofAC-ViconVapor.pdf) [M]
- Aircraft-scale analogues: a Goman-Khrabrov separation-state model combined with bifurcation analysis explains wind-tunnel pitch limit cycles ([ICAS 2020-1104](https://icas.org/ICAS_ARCHIVE/ICAS2020/data/papers/ICAS2020_1104_paper.pdf)). Static normal-force hysteresis in the stall region produced limit-cycle regions that a simple pitch-angle/pitch-rate feedback stabilised ([Southampton eprint 470534](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/470534)). The GK model is a one-state lag on flow attachment ([arXiv 2110.08516](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08516)). [M + Mod, full-scale/airfoil, not gliders]
- Low-Re airfoils: small-amplitude oscillations from laminar separation and large-amplitude ones from dynamic stall (NACA 0012 aeroelastic). For pitching airfoils at stall-regime mean alpha, net energy transfer from flow to airfoil was positive in all cases studied. — [JAFM](https://jafmonline.net/article_1194.html); [Olin](https://phoenixfiles.olin.edu/do/a73b9d96-e051-4e82-a7d3-82e5c9135132) [M/Mod, airfoil pitch DOF, not free flight]

**Free-flight practice (hobbyist)**
- Hand-launch glider trim: set CG for ~15 % static margin (or ~40 % root chord if the NP is unknown). Then trim the elevator until the glider "just will not fly in a straight line without stalling, no matter how slow you throw it". Raising dihedral 12 to 15 deg stopped death dives. — [NAR, McKiou](https://nar.org/?p=2840) [H]
- Moving the CG forward is the standard cure for stalling when conditions get windy/thermally. The alternative, stab TE down, also changes the glide. Moving the CG forward requires more decalage (shim up the stab rear), and a stronger stab allows a more rearward CG. — [Model Aviation "Where's the CG?"](https://www.modelaviation.com/node/1886); [MA FF Duration](https://library.modelaviation.com/article/ff-duration-1) [H]
- "Pack up the [stab] leading edge to correct zooming or stall"; too little decalage risks a loop, too much a dive. — [MA free-flight columns](https://library.modelaviation.com/article/free-flight-old-timers-45) (summary of search results) [H]
- Too far forward a CG makes it hard to get enough tail angle to stop a dive. A slightly nose-up trim sits on the verge of wing stall and glides slowly and "mushy". — [whiteboxlearning glider lesson](https://whiteboxlearning.com/c/application/glider/g1l0501.html) [H]
- No hobbyist source found here used the word "porpoising" or quantified a cure.

### Inferences
- **[Mod, my arithmetic] Linear decay is far too fast to match the flights.** At V = 5.5 m/s, ω_n = √2·9.81/5.5 = 2.52 rad/s, so the Lanchester period is ≈ 2.5 s (the project model gave 3.1 s). Per-cycle amplitude ratio is exp(-2πζ/√(1-ζ²)): 0.57 at ζ = 0.09, 0.47 at ζ = 0.12. After 10 cycles that is 3.4x10^-3 and 5x10^-4. A linearly damped phugoid cannot still show visible swoops after 10 cycles. The persistence must come from (a) a nonlinear sustaining mechanism or (b) continual excitation.
- **[S] Candidate (a): stall-limited bounding cycle, as in Li et al.** Trim alpha ~4-6 deg sits only a few degrees below the ~8 deg slope break (section 1). At the slow crest, alpha rises (from path curvature and any nose-up speed-dependent moment). The plate passes the break, lift collapses, and the CP moves aft (Okamoto & Jinba), giving a nose-down "soft stall". The glider then dives, regains speed, over-rotates and climbs again. Once part of each cycle is spent past the break, quasi-steady linear damping no longer governs amplitude. That fits "small stalls at the top of each swoop" and an amplitude that shrinks onto a floor instead of to zero.
  - Li et al. observed exactly this mode in thin-plate gliders at our Re. Their quasi-steady model also under-predicts how far forward the CoM must be to remove it. That parallels this project's quasi-steady model predicting decay that did not happen.
- **[S] Candidate (b): forced response.** At 5.5 m/s, ordinary outdoor gusts of 0.3-0.5 m/s are 5-10 % speed disturbances at frequencies near the phugoid's 0.3-0.4 Hz. That alone could keep a ζ ≈ 0.1 mode visibly alive, with no limit cycle needed.
  - Discriminating test: a limit cycle has a repeatable amplitude independent of release and of wind, and still appears indoors. A forced response is irregular, correlates with wind, and largely vanishes in still air.
- **[S] Dynamic stall driven by the phugoid's own pitch rate is probably not the main driver.** Phugoid reduced frequency k = ωc/2V ≈ 2.0·0.046/11 ≈ 0.008, well inside the quasi-steady bound. Uhlig & Selig did measure some pitch-up/pitch-down lift asymmetry at k ~0.015, though. At the crest, local separation/reattachment of the LE bubble (time scale of a few c/V ≈ 0.01-0.03 s) can still give a small lag that feeds energy in. Treat it as a secondary amplifier, not the cause.
- **[S] Ranking of passive cures (grounded in Li et al. and hobbyist practice; theory where noted):**
  1. **CG forward / larger static margin (with matching decalage).** This moves the glider from bounding toward steady gliding (Li et al., measured), and hobbyists use it to cure stalling. The cost is a higher sink rate if it is overdone (dive edge at l_CM/l ≈ 0.31 for paper planes).
  2. **Lower trim CL, i.e. fly faster and further from the slope break.** This enlarges the alpha margin to stall, the quantity that sets whether crests stall at all. For a ~8 deg break, trimming at CL ≈ 0.25-0.3 rather than 0.4 buys ~1.5-2 deg (my arithmetic with CL_a ≈ 0.07 /deg).
  3. **More pitch damping (tail volume / longer moment arm).** Classically this barely changes phugoid damping, which is drag-dominated. It does reduce alpha overshoot at the crest, so it should help against a stall-limited cycle. [Mod/S; no low-Re source found]
  4. **Higher wing loading.** Higher V means higher Re (L/D improves from ~6 toward ~8), a longer phugoid period and smaller gust-to-speed ratios. [S]
  5. **Sharpened/chamfered LE on foamboard, and slight camber (5 % arc best below Re 70k per Laitone).** These may delay or soften the slope break. [S, partly M]
- **[S] Correct the model before reusing it.** To test candidate (a), add a stall nonlinearity at the measured ~8 deg break with an aft CP shift, Re-scaled L/D (~6), and optionally a one-state Goman-Khrabrov lag. Then check whether a limit cycle appears at the flown CG and disappears with CG forward. The current linearised quasi-steady model cannot show a limit cycle.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper found that measures or models phugoid–stall limit cycles specifically in small rigid gliders or MAVs, apart from Li et al. 2022 (bounding) and Uhlig & Selig 2013 (hysteresis, no oscillation analysis).
- Not found: phugoid damping measured in free flight at Re < 50k.
- Not found: the dependence of bounding onset on static margin, tail volume or wing loading for tailed gliders (Li's flyers are tailless plates).
- The damping-ratio formula form (1/(√2 L/D) vs 1/√(2 L/D)) was not verified from the primary PDF.
- Hobbyist "porpoising" advice is generic and unquantified. No source gave decalage or CG numbers for ~10 g gliders at Re ~2x10^4.
- Whether Kai's flights were in still air could not be assessed from the literature. That makes candidate (b) impossible to rule in or out here.
