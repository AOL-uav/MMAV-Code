Does it pull out? Released nose-down at rest

An open, source-tagged simulation. Tags: MEASURED (Ananda 2012 wind-tunnel plates), MODEL (AVL, outline only), CALCULATED (masses and inertia from the kit breakdowns), ASSUMED (stated guesses), JUDGEMENT (our definitions). Nothing here is a flight result. The headline number is the first-swoop depth: the height lost at the first time the flight path reaches zero (the first local minimum of height); where the glider never climbs, the height at which the flight path first reaches its final angle.

Verdict: the points that hold up

  • (i) Symmetry. A symmetric plate released exactly nose-down has zero angle of attack and no moment to start pitching: all 18 exactly-nose-down, no-input base cases (three kit wings, every CG tried) dive straight down. With a small disturbance (start at -80 deg, or 0.5 m/s sideways), the kit wings as built (no tab) at the 46 CG settings that sit at least 3% MAC ahead of the neutral point: 46 of 46 still dive (the rest: see the table below). This is the symmetry of the plate plus a CG ahead of the low-angle lift point; it does not depend on the measured tables.
  • (ii) With any nose-up input, the first-swoop depth is about 3 (W/S) / (rho g CL_trim) (an exact relation for a constant lift coefficient and no drag, checked in the tests; the simulation gives a median of 1.25 times it, range 1.16 to 2.06, over 57 cases that are not marked 'cannot say'). At the wing loadings here (4.4 to 13.6 N/m², CALCULATED) that is 2 to 11 m at CL 0.3 to 0.5, and 7 to 34 m at CL 0.10 to 0.15. Equivalently, the depth is 1.5 times the constant-CL radius 2 (W/S) / (rho g CL); the earlier 7 m and 3 m figures were radii, not depths.
  • (iii) A 10 m drop therefore cannot tell "never pulls out" from "needs 30 m" at the forward CG settings, where CL_trim is low and the depth is 7 to 34 m.
  • (iv) Near the neutral point the model cannot say. 423 of the 707 cases are marked 'model cannot say' (within 3% MAC of the neutral point, verdict flips in a sensitivity run, or a bird-outline verdict other than the symmetry result).

Drop height, and whether to add a nose-up input, are Kai's decisions. Nothing on this page recommends a change to the kit tables, the stop rule, the drop height or the hardware.

Where the model cannot say

423 of 707 cases are marked. Reasons (a case can have several): within 3% MAC of the neutral point: 202; verdict flips in a sensitivity run: 13; verdict differs from the base case: 32; verdict flips with: 86; verdict differs from the base kit run: 127; band position not sensitivity-tested: 0; the two disturbed starts disagree: 14; bird outline: 74. Their verdicts are shown greyed and are not part of the points above. Bird-outline first-swoop depths are shown only as "if the AR 5 table carried over" (the bird is AR 9.5; the tested plates stop at AR 5).

Definitions

  • First-swoop depth (headline): height below the release point at the first time the vertical speed returns to zero. If the glider never climbs, the height at which the flight path first reaches its final angle. Not defined for dives and tumbles.
  • Settles to a glide: in the last 2 s the flight path stays within a 10 deg band and is shallower than 45 deg down. The result reports the final angle of attack, glide ratio and the margin to the table's first lift peak (positive = below the peak). Deep stall is reserved for a settled glide more than 10 deg ABOVE the first lift peak (JUDGEMENT; an angle of attack of 29.8 deg against an 11 deg peak counts, 11.9 deg against 11 deg does not).
  • Dives: settles steeper than 45 deg down. Tumbles: pitch angle turns through 360 deg or more. Climbs out, not settled: after the flight path first gets shallower than 20 deg down it never again dives steeper than 80 deg, but has not settled in 30 s. Dives again: anything else.
  • Height to settled is a secondary column: it includes the glide descent while the oscillation decays, so it keeps growing with the glide ratio and is not the pull-out depth.
  • Load factor = aerodynamic force / weight. The constant-CL formula column is 3 (W/S) / (rho g CL_final) with CL_final from the table at the final angle of attack; its ratio to the simulated depth is also listed.

Limits of the model

  • Still air, no quad downwash, no string or release transient, 3 degrees of freedom only. A real release is never exactly nose-down.
  • Quasi-static aerodynamics: no unsteady lift, no added mass.
  • Reynolds number: the tables start at Re 59,999. This glider starts at zero and spends much of the swoop below that range. Everything below is extrapolation.
  • Pitch damping Cmq is AVL's linear small-rate value (bird: at the file's reference point, not at each CG).
  • Height is measured from the release point; the ground is not modelled.

The kit as built: no tab, disturbed starts

This is the regime the flat test is about: no built-in nose-up input, CG ahead of or just behind the low-angle lift point. The exactly nose-down start always dives (symmetry). With a small disturbance (start at -80 deg, or 0.5 m/s sideways), the model gives three behaviours, depending on where the CG is relative to the AVL neutral point:

  • CG well ahead of the neutral point: the disturbance dies out and it keeps diving.
  • A band of CG aft of the neutral point: it swings out of the dive and settles into a slow near-stall glide (numbers below).
  • CG further aft, and the stretch around the neutral point: it wanders, dives again or tumbles; the model cannot say.

How far to trust the band. Its position comes from the centre-of-pressure travel of an unswept, 4.3% thick plate at Re 60 000, applied to a swept, finned, ribbed wing at lower Reynolds number. The edge shifts above (1 mm steps of the kit table limit the resolution) are only the sensitivity to the alternative aerodynamic-centre placement and the measured zero-lift offset range; they do not include the carry-over itself, which is not known. Why is the simulated swoop deeper than the constant-CL formula?

One suspected reason is rotation lag: the glider needs time to swing its nose up while it is already falling and gaining speed, which the constant-CL formula (the body is always along the flight path) does not include. Test: the same forward-CG cases with the pitch inertia halved, with the drag off, and with both.

Halving the pitch inertia changes the difference (sim minus formula) by +1% (F45_span250), +2% (B45_span400). That is not a cut of at least 30% in both cases, so the rotation-lag explanation is not supported and stays unverified by these runs. Switching the drag off cuts the difference by +84% (F45_span250), no defined result (B45_span400); where a run has no defined result the drag-off case no longer settles to the same kind of glide (its verdict is shown), so it is inconclusive there. On these runs drag accounts for most of the difference for the flat wing and is inconclusive for the foam wing; the cause for the foam wing is not established.

Tab / bend effectiveness. The AVL values are inviscid. Scaling the tab (or bend) effect to 75% and 50% of the AVL value changes the first-swoop depth, for the flat-wing cases that settle to a glide in both runs, by a median factor of 1.29 (range 1.03 to 1.40, 30 cases) at 75%, 1.95 (range 1.11 to 2.26, 28 cases) at 50%. Depth scales roughly with 1 / CL_trim, i.e. directly with how much nose-up moment the input really supplies, which is not known for a thin plate at Re 15 000 to 30 000.
