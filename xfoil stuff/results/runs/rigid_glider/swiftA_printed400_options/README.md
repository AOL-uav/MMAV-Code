## What AVL sees

AVL treats the wing as a flat plate (zero thickness, zero camber; `NACA_0000` in `run_case2`) of the outline only. A 2.5-3 mm bead on one face is invisible to it: no thickness, no camber, no leading-edge shape, no drag. So the trim CG, bend angle and neutral point do not change in the model; the only things that change are the mass and its distribution, which this file handles by arithmetic. What the bead could change in reality and AVL cannot say: a rounder leading edge on the lower side (could delay LE separation; no data at this Reynolds number), added pressure drag from a ridge on the windward face, a small camber-like asymmetry, and more spanwise inertia (mass out along the leading edge raises roll and pitch inertia). All unquantified. If we want the effect, it is a flight or CFD question, not an AVL one.
