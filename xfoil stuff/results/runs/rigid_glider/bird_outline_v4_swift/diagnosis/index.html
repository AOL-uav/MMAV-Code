Why the v2 swift-like outline has a broad inner wing and a thin outer wing

Diagnosis only; nothing is rebuilt. AVL outline model (inviscid, body ignored) plus arithmetic. Known caveats: AVL neutral point uncertain by up to about 5% MAC with the panelling when the tail sits in the wake plane; real flat plates lift 8 to 16% above AVL and their aerodynamic centre moves; v2 span efficiency used near-field induced drag (trend only).

Mean chord 26.3 mm (span 250 / aspect ratio 9.5). Arm/hand split at 40% of the semi-span (50 mm); arm leading-edge sweep 5 deg, hand sweep 50 deg. Wing area 6,579 mm²: inner (arm) wing 53%, outer (hand) wing 47%. Fan tail 1,389 mm² (21% of the wing area). Sheet area 8,647 mm²; the nose and the root blend add 679 mm² (8% of the sheet) beyond wing plus tail (v1 outline without blend and with the short nose: 121 mm²). The tail neck is 20 mm wide, the hinge sits 35.8 mm aft of the root leading edge, so the fan starts exactly where the inner wing's trailing edge is; the tail joins the inner wing across 20 mm.

What caused it

1. Parametrisation: the recipe, not the search

  • The chord law was fixed by hand and never searched. The v2 search varied seven switches (m_shape, curved_te, root_blend, tip_round, neck, spread, nose) and the tail bend. The base planform, wrist at 40% of the semi-span, hand leading-edge sweep 50 deg, wrist chord 0.85 x root chord, hand chord falling 1.00, 0.78, 0.50, 0.25, 0.06 of the wrist chord at 0, 40, 75, 93, 100% of the hand, is the v1 BS recipe (all JUDGEMENT; only the aspect ratio 9.5 and the tail spread are CITED). It was never an optimum of anything.
  • The recipe can only express a chord that falls monotonically from the root to a point (a scythe): there is no way to ask for an even or growing chord, or for a different hand chord law other than by editing the station table. With the aspect ratio held at 9.5 the root chord is solved to fill the area, so a pointed hand forces a wide inner wing.
  • The hand sweep of 50 deg is a style choice. The only source in the bird notes is a 60 deg swift hand-wing model in a water tunnel, secondary context, not a planform. The notes give no arm/hand split and no chord law.
  • The fan tail attaches along the inner wing's trailing edge at the root with an 8% span neck (20 mm), so the inner wing is also where the tail, the hinge and the rail saddle live; nothing in the recipe lets the tail attach elsewhere.
  • The root blend and a 9% nose add area at the root only: a small contribution (see the numbers above).

3. Physics and project constraints

  • Real: at aspect ratio 9.5 and 250 mm span the mean chord is only 26 mm; a 0.6 mm printed sheet and a 5 mm foam sheet are 2 to 19% of the chord, so any outer chord of a few millimetres is hard to cut or print and bends easily. The near-tip chord of the v2 BS is 8.5 mm at 95% of the hand. A minimum buildable chord is a real constraint of this project (flat sheet, hand-cut or printed); its value (about 8 to 10 mm is a guess) is JUDGEMENT and not tested here.
  • Real: the fan tail and the hinge need a root region wide enough to take the hinge strip; the tail load (0.4 mm strip) sets the neck width.
  • Inherited/arbitrary: pointed swept hand, 50 deg: copied from the idea of a swift silhouette, not required by passive pitch stability, by the nose-down drop or by a flat sheet. A taper to a point is also a classic way to reduce induced drag at a given span; AVL's span-efficiency numbers here are near-field and only a trend.
  • Not tested by anything here: later folding. A folding wing prefers a moderately wide, simple inner panel, but no folding geometry has been defined.

Findings

  • Free to change: a fuller hand chord law with the same 50 deg sweep (E). Near-tip chord 14 mm instead of 8.5 mm, tip loading 1.06 instead of 1.29, Cn_beta +0.0053 against +0.0047, ballast 12% / 6% against 12% / 7% at an 8% margin. Cost seen: the tail has less authority (0.0032 against 0.0050 CM0 per degree, so about 9.3 deg of bend for a 10% margin against 6.0), and the neutral point moves forward (23% against 31% MAC, partly because the MAC itself moves).
  • Costs something real: less hand sweep (F, H, I). At 30 deg Cn_beta falls to +0.0020 to +0.0023 (the 0.004 floor fails) and the bare CG sits +16% MAC behind the neutral point (against +6%), so ballast rises to 26% printed. Sweep is doing real work against our own yaw-without-a-fin and ballast criteria; it is not just styling. Both criteria are our JUDGEMENT, and a fin or another fix was not tried here.
  • Costs something real: a narrower inner wing (C, D). The hinge sits at the trailing edge of the root chord, so a narrower root pulls the tail forward (hinge 36 mm to 29 mm; tail lever 28 to 20 mm) and the tail loses authority (0.0025 CM0 per degree, a bend of 12 deg for a 10% margin). That coupling is a recipe artefact: tail length and attachment point were not independent parameters.
  • Costs a lot: moving the arm/hand split inboard (G). Margin at 5 deg falls to 3%, a 10% margin needs 16 deg of bend, tip loading rises to 1.37, and the ballast station is out of reach.
  • An even planform (H, I) gives the lowest tip loading (1.01, 0.96 against 1.29) but fails the yaw floor (+0.0023, +0.0027) and the ballast limit (23%, 18% printed) because of the 30 deg sweep chosen with it. The same planform at 50 deg sweep was not run.
  • Which criteria bound the v2 outline? Not ballast (12% printed, 7% foam at an 8% margin). The tip-loading rule favoured rounding the tip. Cn_beta passes by a small margin. The static-margin window sets the bend. The search did not vary the chord law at all, so no criterion chose the broad inner wing; the recipe did.
  • Why the inner wing looks massive: the inner (arm) wing is 53% of the wing area in 40% of the semi-span, with chord 1.36 times the mean at the root, and the visible inner mass also includes the nose and root blend (679 mm²) and the fan tail (21% of the wing area) joined to it through a 20 mm neck.

Suggested degrees of freedom for a v4 recipe (not built)

  1. Hand chord law as two or three free numbers (tip chord fraction, mid-hand fullness) instead of a pointed fixed table, with a stated minimum buildable chord.
  2. Wrist chord / root chord allowed to exceed 1, and the arm/hand split free (not tied to 40%).
  3. Hinge position and tail length independent of the root chord (tail attachment point, length, spread and neck as separate numbers), so the tail lever is not a side effect of the inner chord.
  4. Leading-edge sweep and trailing-edge shape defined separately (so sweep for yaw can be kept without a pointed tip); hand sweep as a searched parameter with the yaw and ballast criteria visible.
  5. Body width, nose and root blend independent of the wing, and counted separately in mass and area.
  6. Keep the criteria as explicit, separately reported numbers; ask for each threshold (margin window, ballast limit, Cn_beta floor, tip loading) whether it is really needed or only inherited.
