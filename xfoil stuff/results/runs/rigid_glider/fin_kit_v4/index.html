Fallback fin kit for swift A and B

Tags: MODEL = inviscid AVL 3.52 (outline only, body ignored; 72 serial runs); ASSUMED = v4 mass assumptions (foamboard 300 g/m², PLA 1.24 g/cm³, inertias from the sheet outline); JUDGEMENT = choices made here. Nothing was flown. Fin effectiveness is uncertain: AVL is inviscid, and fins this small work at chord Reynolds numbers of a few thousand, where real fins usually give less than AVL says. Templates: fin_templates.pdf (1:1 Letter, white).

Options and sizes

Option (a) tip fins: a pair, vertical on the wing top at 95% of the wing, trailing edge level with the wing's. Option (b) centre fin on the body strip, trailing edge 1 mm ahead of the tail hinge line. Option (b) fails in the model (see the sweep): ahead of the hinge the fin's aerodynamic centre sits close to or ahead of the CG, so Cn_beta peaks and then falls as the fin grows. Added as option (c): the same centre fin glued to the tail centreline, aft of the hinge (it moves with the tail bend). Fin height / chord = 1.25 (JUDGEMENT). S1 adds Cn_beta +0.0008 (A reaches 0.004, MODEL), S2 adds +0.0028 (A at 0.006); B gets the same increments. S3 is a build size of 2% of the wing area (JUDGEMENT), well above S1 and S2 to leave room for the low-Reynolds-number knock-down.

Cn_beta, Cl_beta and the NP shift are scale-free; they are about the trim CG at a 10% margin, at CL 0.3, with the tail at the nominal gauge bend (A 6 deg, B 8 deg), so the no-fin values (A +0.0032, B +0.0050) differ from the v4 page (A +0.0030, B +0.0041 at 25% MAC reference and a 5 deg bend). Spiral and Dutch roll are the lateral eigenvalues of AVL's linearised system matrix at the same condition (speed from weight at CL 0.3, mass and inertias from the v4 build, ASSUMED); t2 = time to double. 'New trim CG' = the 10% margin CG at the new neutral point (the shift is the NP shift); the fin mass moves the CG aft as well, so the last column is the nose ballast to add (+) or remove (-) at the v4 nose station to land on the new CG. Fin mass includes a 4 mm glue tab (scaled with span). * chord limited by the local wing chord, so the fin is taller than h/c = 1.25.

Side effects (MODEL)

  • A, foam 400: no fin spiral eigenvalue +0.0010 1/s (neutral); with fins: tip S2 t2 25 s; tip S3 t2 15 s; tail S2 t2 21 s; tail S3 t2 13 s.
  • B, foam 400: no fin spiral eigenvalue +0.0009 1/s (neutral); with fins: tip S2 t2 41 s; tip S3 t2 20 s; tail S2 t2 27 s; tail S3 t2 17 s.

A spiral that doubles in tens of seconds is mild for a glide that lasts a few seconds (JUDGEMENT); whether it matters is for the first drops to show.

Which to try first: (a) tip fins, S3, foam 400 first. In the model they reach Cn_beta 0.004 with 0.23% of the wing area (tail fin 0.27%, similar; centre fin ahead of the hinge never reaches it, best +0.0038) and keep Cl_beta and the spiral mode a little better than the tail fin at equal Cn_beta. S3 gives +0.0095 for A at 0.15 g (CG shift +0.05 mm, nose ballast +0.22 g); it is cut well above the AVL minimum because real fins at this Reynolds number are weaker. The tail fin (c) is the alternative if tip fins are awkward to mount.

Caveats

  • AVL treats each fin as a flat lifting surface in inviscid flow; at chord Reynolds numbers of 1,000 to 8,000 real fins lose lift slope and can stall early, so the model Cn_beta gain is an upper estimate. S1 and S2 are model minimums, far smaller than a fin that is easy to cut and glue.
  • Cl_beta is barely changed (it stays about as stable as without fins). The spiral mode is slightly less stable with fins; the table gives the numbers. Eigenvalues come from AVL's system matrix with an eigen-solver written here (AVL's own eigen routine crashes in this build); inertias are plate estimates, so the Dutch roll damping in particular is an estimate.
  • Fins add mass aft of the CG; foam fin mass assumes the 5 mm foamboard areal mass of the v4 files even for fins this small. The printed numbers use PLA at the v4 plate thickness.
