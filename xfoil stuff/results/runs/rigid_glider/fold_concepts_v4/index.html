Folding concepts on swift outline A (400 mm foam build)

Idea development for choosing from; nothing was built, flown or tested. Outline: v4 A at 400 mm (400 mm span); fold lines: body edge 20 mm, wrist 45.0 mm from the centreline, tail hinge at x = 57.5 mm. Tags: MODEL geometry and arithmetic on that outline, plus v4's AVL strip loads (inviscid); ASSUMED v4's material assumptions (foamboard 300 g/m², PLA 1.24 g/cm³, steel 7.85 g/cm³, band stiffness, profile drag); JUDGEMENT choices made here. The research survey (research/birds/folding_mechanisms.md) ranked concept 1 first, concept 2 (its second) next. Printable white copy: drawings.pdf (concept pages at 1:2 with a scale bar, stowed states at 1:1).

Concept 1: geared single-pivot swing wing

Each wing is one rigid piece on one vertical pin at the body edge; two meshed gear sectors on the lugs make left and right move together; one rubber band opens them against stop pegs; the carrier pin holds them folded through an eyelet in each wing.

Open-pose penalty

Open outline. The swing wing is a cut along the body edge: a 1 mm slit 58 mm long on each side (the hinge gap), and nothing else; no notch is needed in the wing, because the pin sits in a printed lug that lies under the plate. Not free: to fold over the body strip and the tail, each wing plate must sit one plate thickness (5 mm foam) above or below the body plane, and the two wings in different layers (right +1, left -1). In the open pose that is a 5 mm step at each body edge, antisymmetric. With the 0.6 mm printed plate of the 250 mm build the step is 0.6 mm. The hold eyelets add one 8 mm hole in each wing at x 35 mm, y 52 mm (x aft of the root leading edge, y from the centreline; inside the plate). The tail bent up 6 deg rises 5.3 mm at its end, about one plate thick, so it touches the folded wing stack: the tail has to be held flat while the wings are folded.

Hanging folded under the quad

The v4 glider hangs nose-down by a string at the trailing-edge end of the centre rail, which is the hook at x = 54.5 mm. Folded, the wings lie along the body and put 4.6 g behind the hook region: the CG moves +18.6 mm to x = 51.2 mm, still ahead of the hook by 3.3 mm (it must stay ahead, or the glider hangs tail-down). The carrier pin passes through the hook loop and through one 8 mm eyelet in each folded wing: the eyelet sits at x 35 mm, y 52 mm (x aft of the root leading edge, y from the centreline) in the open wing plate (inside the plate, 4 mm clear of the edge). Pulling the pin frees the glider and the wings at the same moment; the band opens them at the lowest speed of the drop. Band tension at the folded end is 4.47 N, which the pin carries in shear.

Concept 2: reversed string, two pieces per side

Arm and hand plates on a shoulder pin and a wrist pin, hand lapped over the arm; sector gears tie left and right at the root; bands open both joints; a leading-edge fold string, tied at the carrier pin through a ring, holds them folded.

Open-pose penalty

Open outline. Two slits: the body edge (58 mm) and the wrist cut (57 mm), each side, 1 mm wide, plus a 12 mm lap where the hand plate lies over the arm plate. The lap is a step of one plate (5 mm foam; 0.6 mm printed) along the whole wrist chord on the top surface; the hand also has to be one layer above the arm, and the wings stack as in concept 1 (plate offset from the body, left and right in different layers). No notch is needed. The wrist pin and the 15 mm pad sit in the lap, a lump on the upper surface. The tail rises 5.3 mm when bent 6 deg: it meets the folded stack here too. The string adds six small guides on the leading edge.

Hanging folded under the quad

Same hook (54.5 mm). Folded CG 48.6 mm, ahead of the hook by 5.9 mm. Here the carrier pin passes through the hook loop and the ring of the fold string; the string is what holds both joints folded against the springs. When the pin is pulled the ring is free, the springs open both joints, and the ring slides forward 21 mm; the string is then slack. Eyelets in the hand plates (x 29, y 53 mm, inside the plate) are the second hold if the string alone is not enough; the arm point (x 27, y 56 mm) is not inside the arm plate.

What does not work geometrically

  • The folded wings are long: tips reach 114 mm past the tail end in both concepts (stowed length 259 mm against 144 mm open), as a swift's do; the arm is only 25 mm of the 180 mm wing, so a second joint does not shorten the stow.
  • Concept 2: the wrist angle that makes the hand lie narrowest is -12.8 deg, i.e. it turns the opposite way to the shoulder; the wrist adds sweep-control freedom later, not stowage.
  • Both: left and right wings overlap (5237 mm2 in concept 1) and lie over the tail, so layers must be offset by a plate thickness; the 5 mm foam plate makes that a visible step in the open outline.
  • Both: the bent-up tail (6 deg) touches the folded stack; the tail has to be held flat while folded.
