Flat-plate balance study from measured data

Analysis of measured wind-tunnel tables only. Every number is tagged: MEASURED (Ananda 2012) = copied from the thesis tables; DERIVED from measured = our arithmetic on those tables; AVL = from our vortex-lattice outline model; EXTRAPOLATED = measured offsets applied to a wing that was not tested. Nothing here is written into the flat-test kit. This is a static moment balance, not a flight result.

What the data supports

Within about 1% of the mean aerodynamic chord (MAC) of the aerodynamic centre, the measured centre of pressure barely moves as the angle changes: on the increasing sweeps it stays within 1% MAC of the aerodynamic centre over a median of 2.9 degrees of angle of attack (range 0.0 to 6.1). A balance point there has almost no restoring moment, so the trim angle is nearly indeterminate. The centre of pressure starts to move aft clearly (increasing sweeps, aspect ratio 3 and up, 27 wings) at a median angle of 8.1 degrees, while the first lift peak (rule in section 4) comes at a median 11.3 degrees (range 9.5 to 13.2); the aft travel therefore begins a median 2.6 degrees before the first lift peak. For aspect ratio 2 the onset angle is offset-dependent and not resolved: AR2_taper0.5 Re 80k up-leg 11.5 deg raw, 7.1 deg with the zero-lift moment removed; AR2_taper0.5 Re 90k up-leg 11.7 deg raw, 10.3 deg with the zero-lift moment removed (aspect ratio 3 and up move by about 0.5 deg or less). Aft of the onset a stable trim exists, but only close to stall. On the AR 3, taper 1, Re 60k plate the aft travel starts at 7.9 degrees with L/D 6.7; the first lift peak is at 11.4 degrees; at 90% of the first-peak lift L/D is 5.6; at the first peak it is 4.4. Across all wings the median L/D at 90% of first-peak lift is 5.7 and at the first peak 4.5. All of this is from unswept, 4.3%-thick plates at Reynolds number 60,000 and above [DERIVED from measured].

1. What this is

A flat plate has no airfoil camber, so where its lift acts (the centre of pressure, CP) changes with angle of attack. This page reads the CP out of measured force and moment tables for ten flat-plate wings. It is a static moment balance only: it says where a balance point could sit under the CP at each angle. It says nothing about whether a glide is steady, and it does not show which balance points glide.

Source: G. K. Ananda Krishnan, M.S. thesis, University of Illinois 2012, Appendix B and C tables (aspect ratio 2, 3, 4 at taper ratio 0.5, 0.75, 1 and aspect ratio 5 at taper 1; thickness 4.3% of chord; unswept; several Reynolds numbers, 60,000 and up) [MEASURED (Ananda 2012)]. 40 runs, 3482 rows. Each run has an increasing-angle and a decreasing-angle sweep; they are reported separately.

Moment reference. Moments are about the quarter chord (thesis PDF p. 32: wings mounted at quarter chord on a wing-tip sting; Table 3.1 "Mc/4"; p. 49: taper was varied about the quarter chord so the quarter-chord line is the same for every wing). The reference chord is the mean aerodynamic chord, 3.5 in for all wings (p. 50). One detail was not found: the thesis equation that turns moment into the coefficient CM. We take it as normalised by that same chord, as the README does. All "% chord" are measured from the leading edge of the mean aerodynamic chord.

Formulas [DERIVED from measured]: normal force CN = CL cos(alpha) + CD sin(alpha); CP position x_cp/c = 0.25 − CM/CN (points with |CN| below 0.05 skipped); L/D = CL/CD. Check: AR 3, taper 1, Re 60k, alpha 4.54: CL 0.277, CD 0.0330, CM 0.0124 gives CN 0.2787, x_cp 0.2055 c, L/D 8.4.

2. The zero-lift moment is not zero

A symmetric flat plate should give zero moment at zero lift. The fitted zero-lift moment (intercept of CM against CL, alpha -4 to 6 deg) is not zero in these runs: it ranges from -0.0084 to +0.0029 [DERIVED from measured]. Whatever the cause (balance offset, sting, tare), the computed CP absorbs it: removing a zero-lift moment CM0 changes the computed x_cp by CM0 / CN. Worked sizes: CM0 = −0.008 moves x_cp by −0.040 chord (4% of chord) at CN 0.2 and −0.020 (2%) at CN 0.4; CM0 = +0.002 moves it by +0.010 (1%) at CN 0.2 and +0.005 at CN 0.4. That is as large as the balance-point differences discussed below, so the study is run twice: raw, and with each sweep's fitted zero-lift moment removed. Both versions are kept in every CSV.

4. Where the centre of pressure goes

Plots: CP position against angle of attack for each wing, one colour per Reynolds number, increasing sweep. Left: raw. Right: fitted zero-lift moment removed. Orange dashed line = the moment reference, 25% chord.

Criteria (parameters at the top of plate_balance.py). Aft travel begins: the baseline is the median x_cp over alpha 2 to 6 deg; the onset angle is the first angle after which x_cp stays at least 2% chord above that baseline up to the first lift peak. First lift peak (this page's "the first lift peak" of earlier versions): the lift slope is fitted at low angle (alpha 2 to 6 deg); scanning upward from 5 deg, the first angle at which the slope over the next two intervals falls below 25% of that low-angle slope ends the rise, and the peak is the highest CL up to the point after it. This finds a true peak (lift then falls by more than 1.5%: marked "peak" in step3_summary.csv) and also the knee where lift stops rising and only creeps up afterwards ("flattening", the plateau seen on several plates). If the slope never falls that far before 20 deg the global maximum up to 20 deg is used ("none"). The earlier version of this page used the global maximum up to 20 deg, which often sat on the post-stall plateau; that value is kept in step3_summary.csv as alpha_global_max_to_20deg. Near-stall trim: the point where lift first reaches 90% of the first-peak lift. CP at the first lift peak: x_cp at the first lift peak. L/D at these points does not depend on the moment, so it is not affected by the zero-lift offset [DERIVED from measured].

Caution on balance points and bands. Balance points for best L/D and bands of balance points can be derived, but they sit inside the zero-lift-offset error and the up/down repeatability error (section 7). In the same way, a statement such as "no positive-lift trim exists ahead of x" depends on the zero-lift offset: the most forward CP moves by CM0/CN. They are therefore not part of the results here.

9. Method notes and limits

  • Fits: alpha -4 to 6 deg; the positive-lift branch starts at CN 0.05; the first lift peak is found by the slope rule in section 4 (fallback: global maximum up to 20 deg). All parameters are at the top of plate_balance.py.
  • Angles are wall-corrected and do not land on round numbers; interpolation is linear between neighbouring tabulated points only.
  • Reference chord for CM is assumed to be the mean aerodynamic chord (section 1).
