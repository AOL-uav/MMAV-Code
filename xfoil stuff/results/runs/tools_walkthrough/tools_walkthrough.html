Tools walkthrough: one mock pass on Swift A, foam 400 mm

This sheet walks through the tools in the order of a design-and-test pass: look up a number in AVL, turn it into a CG and a nose weight, simulate the drop, make the parts, fill in a test-day row, read the video, feed the result back. Each step says what to type or click and what you should see. Every command here was run before it was written down; the screen text is the real output.

Safety rule: work in a practice copy. The project scripts find their folders from where the script file sits. Run from a copy, they read and write only inside the copy, so practice never overwrites the project's results. Do the steps below in PowerShell.

Step 2: AVL by hand on Swift A

AVL is a program you talk to with short commands. Swift A at 400 mm, tail bent 6 degrees, is already written as an AVL file: practice_inputs\SwiftA_foam400_bend6.avl (made from the v4 geometry; its moment reference is the 37.0 mm CG). Type each line, then press Enter. A blank row means press Enter on its own. AVL does not care about capital letters.

#Do thisYou should see
1.\tools\avl\avl352.exeThe AVL banner, a menu, and the prompt AVL c>.
2plop then g then a blank lineTwo small menus; the line Graphics-enable flag F means the plot window is now off (so AVL cannot hang on it).
3load practice_inputs\SwiftA_foam400_bend6.avlConfiguration: SwiftA_foam400_bend6 and the surfaces being built (Wing, Tail).
4operThe operating-point menu, prompt .OPER (case 1/1) c>.
5a c 0.3Sets 'alpha is whatever gives CL = 0.3'. The table shows A lpha -> CL = 0.3000.
6xA few iteration lines, then Total Forces (the block below).
7st then a blank lineEnter filename, or <return> for screen output: the blank line chooses the screen. Then Stability-axis derivatives (below).
8a blank line, then quitBack out of the menu, then AVL closes.

Step 3: from the neutral point to the CG and the nose weight

This is arithmetic, done by walk_balance.py with the same functions the build uses. You type the neutral point you just read (41.713 mm) and the margin you want (0.10 = 10% of the chord).

#Do thisYou should see
1py -B rigid_glider\walk_balance.py 41.713 0.10Five labelled steps (below). Nothing is written to disk.
STEP 1  static margin in millimetres
   MAC (the 'Cref' line on the AVL screen)      = 47.406 mm
   margin 0.10 x MAC                          = 4.741 mm
STEP 2  CG = neutral point - margin
   41.713 - 4.741                        = 36.972 mm behind the root leading edge
STEP 3  the glider before any nose weight (foam sheet + printed rail)
   foam sheet     6.651 g   (sheet area x foamboard areal mass, ASSUMED 300 g/m2)
   printed rail   1.911 g
   together M0    8.563 g, balancing at cg0 = 43.95 mm
STEP 4  how much weight at the nose hole to move the balance point back to the target
   target CG      36.97 mm;  nose weight position (rail hole) -12.5 mm
   weight = M0 x (target - cg0) / (position - target)
          = 8.563 x (36.97 - 43.95) / (-12.5 - 36.97)
          = 1.207 g
   all-up mass    9.770 g   (nose weight is 12.4 % of it)
   wing loading   5.46 N/m2
STEP 5  first-swoop depth estimate (formula, MODEL)
   depth = 3 x (W/S) / (rho x g x CL) = 3 x 5.46 / (1.225 x 9.80665 x 0.3) = 4.55 m
What happenedIn words
Steps 1-2The CG sits one tenth of a chord ahead of the neutral point: 41.713 − 4.741 = 36.97 mm, which the build files round to 37.0 mm (v4's outlines.csv lists 23.1 mm at 250 mm span; ×1.6 = 37.0).
Step 3The foam sheet and the printed rail alone balance at 43.95 mm, behind the target. So weight must go forward.
Step 4Weight needed at the nose hole = M0 × (target − cg0) / (hole − target). Put 1.21 g at the nose hole, 12% of the 9.77 g all-up mass.
Step 5A first guess at the drop: 3 × wing loading / (air density × g × CL). 4.55 m if the lift coefficient stays at 0.3.

Where the sheet's depth range 5 to 18 m for Swift A foam 400 comes from: the formula gives the low end, 4.55 m. The high end multiplies it by the pull-out study's simulation-to-formula ratio for the light foam bird (2.05) and by 1.95 for a tail only half as effective as AVL says: 4.55 × 2.05 × 1.95 = 18.2 m. Swift A is not simulated by the pull-out program (it handles the flat kit wings and an older bird outline). So 5 to 18 m is a formula range, a MODEL estimate, not a prediction of a drop.

Foam sheet mass assumes 300 g/m² foamboard (ASSUMED, to be weighed). The printed rail and the nose hole position come from the v4 build. Your real glider will differ: weigh it and measure the balance point on test day (step 6).

Step 4: the pull-out simulation, one drop

The simulation drops a wing nose-down from rest and follows it for 30 seconds in still air (pitch only). It knows the three flat kit wings: F45_span250, F60_span250, B45_span400. The command takes three inputs: the wing, the CG (mm behind the root leading edge; a whole-mm kit setting) and the tab angle (degrees, trailing edge up).

What to do between drops (the trim guide on page 1 of the sheet)

What happenedAdjustment
Dives / no recoveryCG aft one step, or bend up one gauge step
Porpoise / stall / growing oscillationCG forward one step, or bend down one gauge step
Turns one wayFix warp first, then a small trailing-edge trim
Wanders or spiralsTip fins or a small fin
Swoop deeper than predictedCheck bend on the gauge and CG on the balance

A step is 2 to 3 mm of CG (JUDGEMENT, from the sheet) or the next angle on the field-pack gauge (2, 2.5, 4, 5, 6, 7.5, 8 degrees). Change one thing between drops. Example from the dive in row 1: move the CG one step aft (about 2.4 mm: 37.0 to 39.4 mm) and drop again, as in row 2.

Re-running the arithmetic for a new CG. A smaller margin puts the CG further back. py -B rigid_glider\walk_balance.py 41.713 0.08 (8% margin, the back end of the sheet's model range) gives:

STEP 2  CG = neutral point - margin
   41.713 - 3.793                        = 37.920 mm behind the root leading edge
   target CG      37.92 mm;  nose weight position (rail hole) -12.5 mm

Step 7: CFD (half a page)

What it is for. Checking AVL against the wind-tunnel plate (a flat rectangular plate, Re 60,000) to see whether AVL's pitching moment and centre of pressure can be trusted. It is not run on our wings, and it is not used for balance or pull-out. The runs live in the WSL distro MMAV-CFD; do not start or stop one while another is running.

Status, stop and start. Copied exactly from cfd\validation_sweep\refined_8deg\README.md (PowerShell; I did not run them):

Status: wsl -d MMAV-CFD -u cfd -- bash /home/cfd/run/ref/ref_status_a08.sh
Stop:   wsl -d MMAV-CFD -u cfd -- bash /home/cfd/run/ref/ref_stop_a08.sh
Start:  Start-Process -WindowStyle Hidden wsl.exe -ArgumentList '-d','MMAV-CFD','-u','cfd','--','bash','/home/cfd/run/ref/ref_launch_a08.sh'

The README adds: stop affects only that run; relaunching with the start command resumes from the latest complete time; a hidden Windows wsl.exe keeps the distro alive, so killing it, sleeping, restarting or logging off stops the run.
