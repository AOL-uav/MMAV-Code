Balance points (CG) to mark on the gliders you fly today

How to mark the CG. Every position below is given in millimetres from the NOSE TIP (the frontmost point of the sheet, on the centreline) and also from the TAIL TIP (the rearmost point of the sheet, on the centreline): both are physical edges you can put a ruler on. The distance behind the wing root leading edge (centre LE) is kept as a small secondary line, and the ruler printed on each drawing still counts from the centre LE. Lay the glider flat, nose up the page like the drawing, measure back along the centreline from the nose tip to the green line, draw the line across the span, and balance the glider on a knife edge laid along it.

Swift gliders: the tail is the ONLY thing that bends. One orange hinge line, tail trailing edge UP 6 deg. Do not fold at the body edge or the wrist: those lines on older drawings were markers only and are left off this page.

Prints each glider on its own page, white background, black lines, true size (paper: tabloid/A3 landscape for the 400 mm gliders; set the printer to 100%, no fit to page). Check the scale bar on the print with a ruler before trusting it; if it is not exactly the stated length, the print scaled and the marks are wrong.

Dark screen version below. Drawn at one true mm scale per glider; the ruler on the centreline has ticks every 5 mm and labels every 10 mm. Model values are MODEL (AVL, no thickness or stall), not measurements: the knife-edge balance on the real glider is what counts.

How to measure. Measure along the flat sheet on the centreline, starting at the NOSE TIP (the frontmost point of the sheet, on the centreline); the TAIL TIP is the rearmost point of the sheet on the centreline. Measure from the sheet, not from the printed rail or stick end. This glider has no bend at the centreline, so flat and assembled distances are the same. The printed stick DOES stick out ahead of the sheet nose tip, by 47.4 mm; use it as the zero only if you cannot find the sheet nose (CG then 113.4 mm from the stick front end), but the sheet nose tip is the primary reference.
