# Flat-wing flight test (spec)

Status: test plan and parts. **Nothing here predicts that a flat wing will fly.** Our XFOIL chain cannot model a flat plate
(`results/runs/rigid_glider/plate/summary.txt`). The test exists to find out by flying.

## What and why

Do thin flat wings glide repeatably, and what does a slimmer wing cost? Three specimens, all zero dihedral, all with tip fins of about
4 % of wing area each (24 mm x span/400 tall, chord = tip chord) and a printed fuselage stick:

- **F45** and **F60**, printed, span 250 mm, taper 0.5, quarter-chord sweep 25 deg, plate 0.6 mm (3 layers of 0.2). They differ only
  in aspect ratio (4.5 and 6). F45 is the P07 outline scaled.
- **B45**, foamboard, the F45 outline at 400 mm span (the P07 outline: root 118.5, tip 59.3 mm), one-piece flat wing cut from board,
  no rib. It differs from the printed wings in size, thickness **and** material, so it is its own test, not a third point on the same comparison.

Every number carries a source tag: GEOMETRY (exact), ARITHMETIC (simple sum, inputs stated), AVL (outline only, no section effects),
ASSUMED (stated guess), JUDGEMENT (our choice), PROPOSED (to be agreed), UNKNOWN (to be measured).

### Spar rib (default for printed wings)

The printed wings carry a spar rib: 1.2 mm wide, 2.5 mm high above the plate, along the quarter-chord line of each half (a chevron),
stopping 3 mm short of the tip, with a centre gap of stick width + 0.4 mm so the stick glues flat to the plate. The rib prints on top
(plate flat on the bed) but the wing is **flown rib-down**: rib and stick both underneath. Reasoning (not a measured fact): the upper
surface is where flow separation matters most, so the disturbance goes underneath. Fins are centred on the plate mid-plane (not
including the rib). A plain plate is available with `--plain` and is not generated by default.
With a rib the section is no longer a plain flat plate; it is a flat plate with a spar underneath. Any difference from published
plain-plate tests may partly come from the rib; print one plain wing to separate the two effects.
Stiffness: plate-only against plate+rib uses a T-section second moment of area at the mean chord (ROUGH/ASSUMED, E 3.3 GPa; the real
rib is slanted and shorter, so the real gain is smaller). The mass tables include the rib.

### Optional plain wing

F45-plain (folder `F45_plain_span250`) is the F45 wing with no rib, same fins and stick. The published plate result is for a plain plate, so
one plain wing shows whether the rib changes the behaviour. It is much floppier (the page shows the rough tip-bend figure, ROUGH/ASSUMED).
It is optional and not part of the main comparison.

Foamboard thickness (5.0 mm) and areal mass (300 g/m2 with paper) are ASSUMED, to be measured and weighed (blanks on the page).
5 mm is 5.6 % of mean chord and 8.4 % of tip chord, above the 2.6-4.3 % of the published plate tests; it is also too thick for the
250 mm wings, which is why the foamboard wing is the larger one. Cutting templates are 1:1 SVG on US Letter portrait: one half wing
(centreline marked "flip here for the other half", CG range band and AVL neutral-point line drawn on it) and a fin sheet, each with a
100 mm scale bar. Fins are butt-glued to the tip edge, centred on the board mid-plane (no tabs). Edge shaping (JUDGEMENT): round the
leading edge to about a half-ellipse two board-thicknesses long; taper the last 20 mm of the trailing edge to about 1 mm; same on both
halves; sanding block; check both sides match. The stick is printed and scaled for this wing (166 mm; the page warns above 180 mm).
This craft can be ballasted over a wide range of wing loadings, which the printed ones cannot.

## Test card

**Before flying:** weigh every part (measured column), measure the CG on a knife edge to 0.5 mm, check flatness and twist, photograph.
**CG sweep** (JUDGEMENT): start 15 % of MAC ahead of the AVL neutral point (with fins), 1 mm steps aft to **at least 8 % of MAC behind**
the neutral point; shown as mm aft of root leading edge and mm ahead of (+) or behind (-) the neutral point, with each setting marked
ahead or behind. Places to try, not a prediction. Each wing's bare-aircraft balance point (wing, fins and stick, no ballast; ARITHMETIC on
ASSUMED masses) is printed beside its table; the stick ticks and the ballast table cover the whole extended table.
**Stop rule** (JUDGEMENT): after the printed table, continue aft in 1 mm steps while any setting still glides or still dives; stop when,
after at least one gliding setting, two consecutive settings give no steady glide; or when the bare-aircraft balance point is reached
(beyond that, ballast must go in the holes under the wing instead of the nose, a different build). As the balance point moves aft the
nose ballast gets smaller, so the glider also gets lighter across the sweep: weigh it at each setting.
**Releases:** 5 per setting; 10 at any setting that glides (JUDGEMENT).
**Outcome vocabulary (exact):** steady glide, dive, pitch-up or stall, pitch oscillation, tumble, roll-off left, roll-off right, yaw wander.
*pitch oscillation* = repeated swooping or bounding, nose up and down; *tumble* = flips end over end. The old bare word "oscillation" is
not used: the report script flags such rows and asks for "pitch oscillation" or "yaw wander" instead of guessing.
**Proposed pass rule (PROPOSED):** at least 8 of 10 steady at one setting, and both 1 mm neighbours also glide (at least half steady).

### Ballast without clay

A row of 3.2 mm square holes (M3 clearance) at 5 mm pitch along the nose and under the wing: M3 screws and nuts. Fine trim: a washer or
coin taped on and slid along the stick. Nominal masses, ASSUMED, weigh yours: US penny 2.5 g, dime 2.27 g, nickel 5.0 g; M3 hardware
UNKNOWN. The CG measured on the knife edge is what counts, not the ballast table.

### Drone drop (the launch method)

Procedure, not prediction; we do not claim either release works.

1. Hang the glider **level** from a thread loop round the stick and over the wing root, at the balance point. The 5 mm marks on the
   stick are wider notches that seat the loop; the nose hole is for the pin or hook.
2. Release: (1) **open wire hook released by a sharp acceleration or deceleration of the quad**: cheapest, try first; release attitude
   and speed will vary, so note what the quad was doing; (2) **pin release**: more repeatable, for later.
3. Release in forward flight at roughly the glide speed for the wing loading (page table, ARITHMETIC, from the measured mass), **not from a hover**.
4. Same height every time, 10 m suggested (JUDGEMENT), calm air, a dummy drop first to check the glider clears the props, bright colour.
5. Why height matters (ARITHMETIC): pitch-bob (phugoid) period T = pi x sqrt(2) x V / g, about 0.45 x V seconds; several seconds of flight
   are needed to see whether it settles.
6. Record from ground video: time to ground, distance, heading change (notes), outcome; release height and speed.

Hand launch is for gross trim only (1-2 s of flight sorts dive from pitch-up; cannot show a repeatable glide). The log has `launch_method`
(free text, e.g. "drone drop, open hook", "hand") and release_height_m / release_speed_mps columns; the report never pools launch methods.

### What each outcome would mean (conditional)

- No steady glide at any setting: this counts only after the stop rule has been followed past the AVL neutral point. Then a clean flat wing
  of this size does not trim itself on its own at the settings tried; try a lip next (a different test).
- Gliding at only one setting: range narrower than 1 mm; use 0.5 mm steps. Several in a row: a range exists; compare with the AVL neutral point.
- Going aft: dive, then glide, then pitch oscillation, then tumble is the order reported in the published plate study (Li et al. 2022,
  unswept plates). Seeing it would be the same pattern; it would not say our wings behave like those plates in other ways.
- Pitch-up or stall behind the neutral point: on a swept, tapered wing the tips may let go first as the nose rises (unknown). Check twist.
- Pitch oscillation is about the balance point and the wing; do not blame the fins. Yaw wander (nose swings side to side) points at the fins.
  Tumble: in the published study it appeared with the centre of mass furthest aft.
- F45 works and F60 does not: slimmer costs repeatability at this thickness, size and rib. A printed wing glides and the rib matters to
  you: fly the optional F45-plain wing the same way (the published plate result is for a plain plate).
- B45 glides or not: says something about 5 mm foamboard at 400 mm only; size, thickness and material all differ from the printed wings.

## What we know and do not know

**Published free-flight result (Li, Goodwill, Wang & Ristroph 2022, J. Fluid Mech. 937, A6, doi 10.1017/jfm.2022.89, open access).** Quoted in its
own terms (unswept rectangular plates, fraction of chord); it says nothing about our wings.
- Paper sheets in air, 15 cm span x 5 cm chord, weighted leading edge, small side fins. Centre of mass from mid-chord toward the leading
  edge, as a fraction of chord: 0 and 0.08 tumbled; 0.14 unsteady (bounding); 0.24 glided; 0.31 dived. From the leading edge that is
  50 %, 42 %, 36 %, 26 %, 19 % of chord (ARITHMETIC: 0.5 minus the value).
- Plastic plates in water, 20.3 cm span x 2.54 cm chord x 0.15 cm thick, unswept rectangles, Reynolds number about 1,000 to 10,000: steady
  gliding for a centre of mass 0.22 to 0.29 ahead of mid-chord, i.e. 21 % to 28 % of chord from the leading edge (ARITHMETIC).
- Stated mechanism: the centre of pressure on the plate moves with angle of attack (attached to separated flow); a steady glide is where it
  coincides with the balance point.
- Ananda, Sukumar & Selig 2015 give an aerodynamic centre of 20 to 27 % of chord at low angle of attack, moving aft toward stall (read from
  the first 13 of 15 pages of the paper).
- What we do not know: (a) that study used unswept rectangles at a lower Reynolds number than ours; (b) on a swept, tapered wing the tips may
  let go first as the nose rises, which would make settings behind the neutral point pitch up rather than glide (unknown); (c) our printed
  wings have a spar rib, the study's plates did not; (d) so this is a reason to keep testing past the AVL neutral point, not a prediction.

Published plate tests (Ananda, Sukumar & Selig 2015, 2.6-4.3 % plates, AR 2-5, unswept) stop at Re 60,000; these wings fly at about
12,000-31,000 (default plate/board, ARITHMETIC). No data on a lip on a plate. Our model has no plate result. AVL knows only the outline,
not the rib or the thickness. Unknown until measured: real masses, real CG, print and board quality, whether any setting glides.

## Limits

Lips, tails, dihedral joints and other spans are out of scope. No CG window, glide ratio or "will fly" claim is made for any plate.
