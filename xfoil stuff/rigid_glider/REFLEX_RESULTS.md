# Local fixed-reflex branch: screening result

This report is for the bounded study in `reflex_config.json`, not a final
MMAV size or an L/D optimization. Commands, raw decks, XFOIL polars, and
machine-readable results are described in `REFLEX_STUDY.md`.

**Bottom line:** At the wing's predicted low-Re, low-Cl operating
conditions, XFOIL predicts that small reflex lips retain only a fraction of
the pitching-moment authority predicted by inviscid AVL, while larger lips
retain more but remain strongly condition-dependent. Neither prediction has
been experimentally validated, and the same low-Re uncertainty also affects
the bare symmetric wing used in the tail configuration.

**Amended 2026-10-09** after an audit of section C and an extension study
(`reflex_ext_*.py`). The original numbers are unchanged. What was checked:
the tables in sections A, B and D against `static.csv` and `tolerances.csv`;
the section C tables recomputed from the raw polars
(`reanalysis/audit/out1.txt`); two AVL cases and one XFOIL polar re-run with
zero difference (`repro_comparison.csv`, `xfoil_repro.json`); and five
section-coordinate and command files regenerated byte-identical
(`reanalysis/audit3_out.txt`). The amendment corrects the wording of the main warning, adds
the static-margin, spanwise-extent, tolerance and extra-lip results, and
corrects three grid cases wrongly left without a trim. Section H, added the
same day, holds the accepted section-experiment matrix and the model
predictions for it. Extension results are
in `results/runs/rigid_glider/reflex_ext/`; the audit and re-analysis tables
cited below are in its `reanalysis/` subfolder.

Three kinds of statement are kept apart throughout:

- **AVL (inviscid):** what the vortex-lattice model predicts with no viscous
  effects. All trim CL, static margin, tolerance and placement numbers in
  sections A, B, D and E are of this kind.
- **XFOIL at the wing's local Cl and Re:** what the 2D viscous section model
  predicts where the wing actually operates (section C).
- **Section H** contains both kinds of prediction, for lips larger than
  those in sections A to D, and is labelled line by line.
- **Experimentally unknown:** neither model is validated here. Whether a
  printed lip produces any of these moments at Re 60-100k is not established.

## A. Minimum reflex in the AVL static screen

The reference is a planar NACA 0008 wing: span 1.2 m, AR 7, taper 0.65,
quarter-chord sweep 25 degrees, S=0.2057 m², MAC=0.1740 m. W/S=8 N/m²,
rho=1.225 kg/m³, nu=1.5e-5 m²/s. CG is set near 10% MAC ahead of AVL Xnp
for each sweep. The 0.2–0.5 trim-CL and 5–15% static-margin bands are
exploratory. The bounded grid contains 135 cases (15/25/35-degree sweep,
three chord fractions, five angles, three placements); 41 pass those
illustrative bands in inviscid AVL.

Correction: `static.csv` leaves three of the 135 cases without a trim (full
span, 15% chord, 5 degrees, at each sweep). That was a probe-range artifact:
Cm was still positive at the top of the -2 to 8 degree alpha sweep. With the
probe widened they trim at alpha 8.8-9.0 degrees with CL 0.502, 0.488 and
0.462 for 15, 25 and 35-degree sweep (`reflex_ext/item6_notrim.json`). Two
of those fall numerically inside the bands, but an alpha near 9 degrees is
close to low-Re stall for these sections, so they are not counted as usable
passes and the 41 is left as it is.

At 25-degree sweep and 10% margin, full-span trailing-edge-up features first
reach CL>=0.2 in the discrete tested angles at:

| Lip chord | Angle | AVL trim CL | Trim alpha | Lip rise at root/tip |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5% | 4 deg | 0.253 | 4.44 deg | 0.72 / 0.47 mm |
| 10% | 3 deg | 0.260 | 4.62 deg | 1.09 / 0.71 mm |
| 15% | 3 deg | 0.296 | 5.36 deg | 1.63 / 1.06 mm |

The 15%-chord, 2-degree case yields CL=0.198 at 25 degrees—essentially the
edge of the illustrative band—and CL=0.203 at 15-degree sweep. There is no
unique "minimum" across chord, height, angle, and spanwise coverage. The
original focused viscous polars covered the 10%-chord, 3-degree lip only;
the extension added 5%/4, 10%/5, 15%/2 and 15%/3-degree lips on NACA 0006
and 0008 (section C). None of it is validation. The
5%-chord result is especially mesh-sensitive: at alpha=4.5 degrees, its AVL
Cm changes from 0.00329 to 0.00687 to 0.00851 with 12, 20, 32 chordwise
panels. The 10%-chord case changes much less (Cm -0.00178, -0.00010,
0.00039), so treat 5% thresholds as coarse.

The 10%-chord, 3-degree full-span comparison case has AVL trim CL=0.2596,
alpha=4.62 degrees, Xnp=0.17816 m, CG=0.16068 m, SM=10.04%,
Cm_alpha=-0.4299/rad, Cl_beta=-0.02794/rad, Cn_beta=+0.00226/rad. It needs V=7.09 m/s
at the assumed loading (Re root/tip≈98k/64k). Both it and the untwisted
zero-reflex symmetric wing have no useful inherent directional stability.

These are **inviscid AVL trim equilibria**, not confirmed physical equilibria.

## B. Spanwise placement

At 25-degree sweep, 10%-chord/3-degree feature: full-span CLtrim=0.260;
outboard 55–100% semi-span CLtrim=0.196; inboard 0–55% semi-span
CLtrim=0.064. The outboard span contributes much more pitch authority than
the inboard span, consistent with its aft position on the swept wing, but it
needs an abrupt feature start and offers no clear robustness advantage here
(the extension below finds a modest inviscid advantage in mismatch roll and
lip-angle tolerance, subject to the section C caveat).
The full-span lip is a continuous straight hinge line in this linear-taper,
linear-sweep planform and is the simplest of the tested arrangements. For an
outboard-only 10%-chord lip, 4 degrees gives CLtrim=0.261 at 25-degree sweep.

- An outer 30-100% lip matches the full-span trim CL with about two thirds

- **Caveat from section C:** the outboard sections have the smallest chord
  and Reynolds number and a low local Cl, which is where XFOIL gives the lip
  the least moment authority. The inviscid advantage of outboard placement
  is therefore the part of this study least likely to survive viscous
  effects.

## C. Viscous cost and credibility

Generated NACA 0004 (thin proxy), 0006, 0008, and 0010 baseline and
10%-chord/3-degree bent sections were run at Re=40, 60, 80, 100, 130, 160k.
Of 1,104 requested alpha points, 1,077 were returned; missing points cluster
mainly in NACA 0004. This is numerical coverage, **not validation**.
Reversing the alpha-sweep direction for the bent 0008 section at 60/80/100k
returned the same rounded CL and Cm at 0, 2, and 4 degrees; no
sweep-direction hysteresis was found in that limited check.

At Re=80k and matched section Cl=0.3:

| Section | Clean Cd | Bent Cd | Clean Cm | Bent Cm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| NACA 0004 proxy | 0.01552 | 0.01774 | +0.0007 | +0.0166 |
| NACA 0006 | 0.01201 | 0.01246 | -0.0071 | +0.0051 |
| NACA 0008 | 0.01289 | 0.01308 | -0.0148 | -0.0062 |
| NACA 0010 | 0.01417 | 0.01432 | -0.0259 | -0.0165 |

The NACA 0006 is lower drag than 0008 here. The 0004 proxy yields the
largest positive bent-section Cm, but has much higher Cd and the most missing
polar points. No section ranking is robust across all Re/Cl. A literal
zero-thickness plate was not fed to XFOIL.

**Main warning:** with the same 0008 bent coordinates at alpha=0, inviscid
XFOIL predicts CL=-0.138 and Cm=+0.0291; viscous XFOIL at Re=80k predicts
CL=+0.008 and Cm=-0.0018. The inviscid AVL wing at alpha=0 predicts
CL=-0.0866 and Cm=+0.0347, so the two inviscid models agree on the sign of
the feature's effect. In viscous XFOIL the lip's moment benefit is largely
lost at the low local lift coefficients corresponding to the predicted trim
condition, and returns only partially at higher Cl and at Re 130–160k (the
tables below quantify this). This is a large
model/physics discrepancy exactly where trim is needed. Low-Re transition,
separation, sharp-bend paneling, and XFOIL branch/convergence behavior are
credible contributors. A returned polar row does not establish the real flow.
Consequently, the AVL-positive trim is **not** evidence that this small lip
will physically trim; XFOIL's viscous Cm cannot simply be added to AVL Cm
without a consistent 3D model.

### Lip authority where the wing actually operates (XFOIL, re-analysis)

The comparison has to be made at matched local Cl, not at matched alpha: the
wing trims at 4.6 degrees, but a 2D 0008 section reaches the same Cl at
about 2 to 2.5 degrees. From the AVL strip forces at the nominal trim
(`reflex_ext/reanalysis/task3_strips.csv`), 92% of the wing area runs at
local Cl 0.20–0.30 with none above 0.285, and strip Re runs from 64k at the
tip to 98k at the root (40% of area at 60–80k, 60% at 80–100k).

Viscous lip moment (bent minus clean Cm at the same Cl) as a fraction of the
inviscid value, NACA 0008, 10%-chord 3-degree lip
(`reflex_ext/reanalysis/task1_matched_cl_10_3.csv`):

| Re | Cl 0.2 | Cl 0.3 | Cl 0.4 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 40k | 2% | 4% | 27% |
| 60k | 2% | 11% | 48% |
| 80k | 5% | 30% | 50% |
| 100k | 9% | 45% | 52% |
| 130k | 22% | 57% | 52% |
| 160k | 27% | 62% | 51% |

The wing occupies only the Cl 0.2–0.3 columns in the 60k–100k rows, so
XFOIL gives the lip between about 2% and 45% of the authority AVL used to
size it, lowest outboard. It never reaches the Cl 0.4 column. The AVL trim
point is itself a product of the inviscid authority, so with less authority
the trim moves to lower CL, where the loss is larger. At fixed section alpha
of 3 to 6 degrees the lip retains about a third of its inviscid moment at
all Re, but the wing does not operate there at this trim.

The other lips were run on NACA 0006 and 0008 to see whether a larger lip
escapes this (`reflex_ext/xfoil/`, 1,099 of 1,104 viscous points returned).
Same fraction for NACA 0008 at Re 60k / 80k / 100k
(`reflex_ext/reanalysis/task2_ratio_table.csv`):

| Lip chord / angle | Cl 0.2 | Cl 0.3 | Bent-minus-clean Cd at Cl 0.3, 80k |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 5% / 4 deg | .00 / .00 / .05 | .07 / .22 / .37 | +0.00010 |
| 10% / 3 deg | .02 / .05 / .09 | .11 / .30 / .45 | +0.00019 |
| 10% / 5 deg | .03 / .10 / .21 | .22 / .40 / .51 | +0.00061 |
| 15% / 2 deg | .06 / .10 / .11 | .12 / .30 / .46 | +0.00017 |
| 15% / 3 deg | .06 / .13 / .17 | .19 / .39 / .52 | +0.00048 |

No tested lip escapes the loss: at Re 60k and Cl 0.3 the best retains 22%,
and at Cl 0.2 nothing exceeds 21% anywhere in the 60k–100k band. Larger
chord and angle help somewhat and the 5% chord is consistently worst, but
there is no clean scaling law, so these data do **not** support sizing a lip
that would trim. Section H extends this to much larger lips, where XFOIL
does predict a substantial section moment. The profile-drag cost of the lip is small for every 0008 lip in
this table (the 0004 proxy above shows +0.0022); it is the moment authority,
not the drag, that is in doubt.

### The clean section is not moment-neutral either (XFOIL, re-analysis)

AVL treats the symmetric section as having zero moment about quarter chord.
XFOIL at Re 60k–100k gives clean NACA 0008 a nose-down Cm of -0.004 to
-0.017 across Cl 0.2–0.4, with nose-up excursions at lower Cl and Re and
above about 4.5 degrees. The slope dCm/dCl is -0.05 to -0.09 for Cl 0.1–0.3
(section aerodynamic centre aft of quarter chord, stabilising) and +0.07 to
+0.09 for Cl 0.35–0.55 (forward, destabilising), reversing at Cl of about
0.29–0.35 for the clean section and 0.20–0.28 with the lip
(`reflex_ext/reanalysis/task4_ac_shift.csv`, `task4_sign_changes.csv`). With
the wing's maximum local Cl at 0.285, the clean-section reversal sits just
above the operating band and the with-lip reversal inside it. A
shift of 5–9% chord is the same size as the 10% design margin, so the AVL
static margins in this report are inviscid statements only. This applies
equally to the planar 0008 main wing of the tail branch; it is not a point
against the lip specifically.

### What remains unknown

- Whether XFOIL's low-Re result is physical. The polars are irregular (for
  clean 0008 at 80k, CL is 0.288 at 2 degrees and 0.475 at 4 degrees), the
  boundary layer is laminar to the trailing edge at alpha 0, consistent with
  laminar separation, and XFOIL's re-paneling may round the sharp bend. That
  last point was not tested.
- How 2D section moments combine on the swept 3D wing. No consistent 3D
  viscous model exists here.
- Real behaviour of a printed lip, including surface roughness and bend
  radius. Only a measurement resolves this.

For the AVL 25-degree/10%-chord/3-degree trim only, local-Cl/Re strip
integration gives XFOIL profile CD≈0.01298, AVL induced CD≈0.00312, and
conditional L/D≈16.1. This number assumes the AVL trim exists and excludes
other airframe drag; it must not decide the architecture.

## D. Manufacturing robustness

For that full-span case, one-at-a-time AVL perturbations give:

| Perturbation | Trim CL range | Residual rolling Cl (nominal +0.00067) |
|---|---:|---:|
| CG ±5 mm | 0.202–0.363 | small, <0.001 |
| Lip angle ±0.5 deg | 0.216–0.302 | <0.001 |
| Lip angle ±1 deg | 0.173–0.345 | <0.001 |
| Lip chord 8–12% | 0.235–0.275 | <0.001 |
| Left/right lip mismatch ±1 deg | 0.258–0.261 | -0.00206 to +0.00340 |
| Left/right incidence mismatch ±1 deg | 0.254–0.265 | -0.00603 to +0.00736 |

The nominal case falls below the illustrative CL=0.2 lower edge with a
1-degree *reduction* in lip angle. The ±5 mm CG range just stays within it.
Wing-incidence mismatch produces roughly 2–3 times the rolling moment of
equal-size lip-angle mismatch, and neither creates yaw restoring stability.
These are independent, exploratory errors; dynamics were not tested, and
combined errors are covered in the extension subsection below. The small nominal roll offset of +0.00067 appears in this
explicit two-half AVL mesh and should be subtracted when interpreting deltas.
The extension confirmed it is a model artifact and not a lip effect: the
same wing with no lip shows a roll coefficient of about 0.0025 x CL
(`reflex_ext/raw/np_s25/`), and the left and right strip meshes are not
mirror images. The cause was inferred, not diagnosed.

### More cases and combined errors (AVL, extension)

The same one-at-a-time perturbations were repeated for other lips, with
denser CG and lip-angle scans, to find how much error is tolerated before
trim CL leaves 0.2–0.5 (`reflex_ext/item4_limits.csv`,
`reflex_ext/reanalysis/task5_roll_slopes.csv`). All at 25-degree sweep and
10% margin:

| Case | CG tolerated | Lip-angle error tolerated | Roll per deg of L/R lip mismatch |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Full span 10% / 3 deg | -5.3 to +8.4 mm | -0.69 deg | 0.0027 |
| Full span 5% / 4 deg | -4.7 to +8.6 mm | -0.85 deg | 0.0019 |
| Full span 15% / 3 deg | -8.4 to +7.1 mm | -0.98 deg | 0.0033 |
| Outer 55–100% 10% / 4 deg | -5.4 to +8.3 mm | -0.94 deg | 0.0016 |

- Trim CL is always the binding limit, never static margin, which stays
  between about 7% and 13% over CG ±5 mm. A steeper lip of up to +2 degrees
  stays in band in every case.
- A left/right wing-incidence mismatch gives 0.0067 roll per degree
  regardless of the lip, about 2.5 times the full-span lip mismatch and
  about 4 times the outboard one. At ±2 degrees it reaches ±0.013.
- Combined worst-case corners for the nominal full-span 10%/3-degree lip
  (CG ±5 mm, lip angle ±1 degree, mismatch ±1 degree;
  `reflex_ext/item4_corners.csv`): two of the eight leave the CL band. With
  the CG 5 mm forward and the lip 1 degree shallow, trim CL falls to 0.13.
  Static margin stays within 7–13% in all eight.

These tolerances are inviscid. Given section C, the real sensitivity to lip
angle is unknown and could be either smaller, if the lip is ineffective, or
less predictable.

## E. Tail versus reflex

The retained adjustable-tail reference uses the same planar main wing, an
8%-area tail at xAC=0.44 m, tail incidence -3 degrees, and about 10% static
margin. It trims at CL=0.288, V=6.74 m/s, with CG ±5 mm giving CL=0.224–0.403.
At 6% tail area and the same arm, CLtrim=0.232; 10% area gives 0.337. A
3%-area center fin at xAC=0.48 m raises AVL Cn_beta from about +0.002 to
+0.012/rad. The tail's estimated Re is only ~18k, below the focused/old
polar range, so a fair total L/D comparison is **not available**. Tail
incidence ±1 degree yields CL≈0.192–0.383: it, too, needs alignment, but is
readily adjustable between tests.

The lip uses fewer parts and no separate tail mount. Its full-span bend must
be replicated within roughly fractions of a degree to hold the nominal trim
band, and its low-Re moment authority is unverified. The fixed tail needs a
boom/mount and alignment, but area, arm, CG and incidence can be changed on
one test article. Both branches still need yaw stabilization and measured
release/dynamic behavior. Neither has a validated L/D advantage here.

The tail branch's trim CL of 0.288 is as inviscid as the lip's, and its
planar 0008 main wing carries the same viscous section-moment nonlinearity
described in section C. The tail's advantage is that its trim mechanism does
not depend on a small trailing-edge feature working at low Cl and low Re,
and that incidence, area, arm and CG can be adjusted on one article. It is
not immune to the low-Re uncertainty.

## F. Later wing reversal/reorientation

A fixed upward lip is directional: flipping the wing relative to incoming
flow reverses which face/edge carries the protrusion and can reverse its
pitching effect or make it an exposed leading-edge feature. It can also snag
folding clearance. This is a geometric consequence, not a samara simulation
or automatic rejection.

## G. First physical branch

Use the retained **adjustable planar wing + small fixed tail** as the first
passive-flight reference article, with optional simple fin. It offers a
measurable and changeable trim mechanism while the lip's moment authority at
the wing's local Cl and Re is predicted by XFOIL to be 2–45% of what AVL
assumed. Keep the reflex branch alive for a cheap
section/lip validation experiment and a later interchangeable wing—not yet a
committed pure-wing airframe. The highest-value next evidence is measured
low-Re pitching moment/lift for a printed clean and bent section around
Re=60–100k, then repeatable drop tests with measured CG and wing incidence.

**Decision (Kai, 2026-10-09):** the tail branch is the first full flight
prototype, as the only architecture here with a reasonably credible path to
positive-lift trim. The reflex branch is not abandoned: the section
validation experiment goes ahead first, and is to be aggressive enough to be
informative rather than limited to NACA 0008 with a 10%-chord 3-degree lip,
which the analysis already rates marginal to bad. The experiment's design
is not fixed by this report.

### What the predictions say, and what they do not

- In XFOIL the near-total collapse of lip authority at low Cl is specific
  to small lips. Large lips still lose roughly half to three quarters of
  their inviscid moment in the operating cells: the 15% / 8-degree lip
  gives a lip moment (bent minus clean) of +0.02 to +0.05 across Cl 0.2–0.3
  and Re 60k–100k, which is 0.25 to 0.56 of inviscid.
- Part of that better fraction comes from where the comparison lands. A
  large lip costs so much lift that Cl 0.25 needs alpha 3.6 to 4.8 degrees,
  where the authority has returned as upper-surface transition moves
  forward. At a fixed alpha of 0 the retained fractions at Re 80k are only
  0.10 (15% / 5), 0.20 (15% / 8) and 0.35 (20% / 10).
- Equal section Cm at one Cl is not equal trim capability. The net bent
  section Cm for 15% / 8 on NACA 0008 (clean moment plus lip moment) runs
  from +0.018 at Re 60k, Cl 0.2 to +0.032 at Re 100k, Cl 0.3, which
  brackets the +0.029 that AVL's nominal 10% / 3-degree lip is worth
  inviscidly. But the inviscid figure is nearly flat in Cl, while the
  viscous 15% / 8 section has dCm/dCl of +0.05 to +0.09 and only +0.015 to
  +0.017 at alpha 0 to 2. This is **not** a 3D trim prediction and not a
  lip sizing.
- The larger lips cost drag (15% / 8 adds about 17% to section profile
  drag at this cell) and move the section aerodynamic centre forward by
  3–9% chord, which is the same size as the 10% design margin.
- Inviscid AVL, for reference: full-span 15% / 5, 15% / 8 and 20% / 10 lips
  at 10% margin trim at CL 0.49, 0.76 and 0.98 with alpha 8.9, 14 and 19
  degrees, all outside the usable range. The inviscid lip angle for trim CL
  0.26 is about 2.6 degrees at 15% chord and 2.5 degrees at 20%. XFOIL
  retains between 2% and 56% of the inviscid lip moment across the tested
  0008 lips and operating cells; no XFOIL-based figure for the lip needed
  exists.

### Reliability of these predictions

- Viscous runs returned 589 of 621 points. The 20% / 10-degree NACA 0008
  polar at Re 100k is unusable (5 of 23 points); single points are missing
  inside the working range for 0006 15% / 8 at 80k and for the 0004 proxy.
- The polars are as irregular as the earlier ones, in clean and bent
  sections alike. The polar files do not show where the flow separates; a
  boundary-layer dump (skin friction and shape factor) would, and none was
  taken.
- In every large-lip polar on NACA 0006 and 0008, at all three Re, the
  lower surface is laminar to the trailing edge for alpha 0.5 to 8 degrees
  (bottom transition at x/c 1.0). A laminar boundary layer carried through
  an 8 or 10-degree corner is the condition XFOIL models least reliably.
  At Re 80k the pressure drag rises by 50–100% and the lift-curve slope
  falls to 0.097–0.106 per degree against 0.117 inviscid.
- Several 20% / 10-degree cases (NACA 0006 at 80k and 100k, and the 0004
  proxy) show a moment almost independent of Cl. Do not read that column
  closely.
- Re-paneling of the bend was again not tested (now tested; see the
  2026-10-11 addendum below).
- The experiment should be read lip by lip and quantity by quantity (lip
  moment, clean-section moment, dCm/dCl and lift) against both the XFOIL
  and the inviscid columns. Results may fall between the two, below XFOIL,
  or follow one model for small lips and the other for large ones. A lip
  moment near the inviscid column would not by itself confirm the AVL trim,
  because AVL also sets the clean section's moment and its slope to zero.

### Addendum 2026-10-11: paneling sensitivity, boundary-layer dumps, recovered polar

A numerical-sensitivity study, not validation. Code is
`reflex_ext_gaps.py` and `reflex_ext_gaps2.py`; outputs are in
`results/runs/rigid_glider/reflex_ext/gaps/` and `gaps2/`. The stored
15% / 8 and clean NACA 0008 polars at Re 80k were reproduced exactly before
anything was varied. The numbers in the tables above are unchanged; they
are the 160-panel baseline.

**The XFOIL lip moments above are not paneling-converged.** NACA 0008, lip
moment (bent minus clean Cm at matched Cl, clean from the same paneling), as
a change from the 160-panel baseline. Three tiers of evidence, not to be
mixed:

| Tier | Variant | 10% / 3 | 15% / 8 | 20% / 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clean refinement | 240 panels, at Cl 0.25, Re 80k | +26% | +7% | +6% |
| Clean refinement | 240 panels, range over Cl 0.20–0.30, Re 60k–100k | +10 to +58% | +4 to +15% | +4 to +8% |
| Hinge-only refinement | 160 panels bunched at the hinge, same range | −18 to −2% | −5 to −1% | −3 to −1% |
| Trend only | 320 panels, at Cl 0.25, Re 80k | +48% | +11% | +9% |
| Node distribution | input cosine spacing used as-is, about 160–220 nodes, range | +25 to +209% | +10 to +51% | +11 to +26% |

- The 240-panel tier is free of known numerical caveats and by itself shows
  the baseline is not converged.
- Every 320-panel run triggered XFOIL's wake-array truncation warning. It
  shows the trend continuing; its magnitudes should not be quoted.
- The as-is tier is a change of node distribution at a similar count, not a
  refinement. The finest as-is set (321 nodes) failed outright (23 of 276
  points), and the 161-node as-is set lost 6 of 23 alphas on the 20% / 10
  lip. There is no evidence the as-is values are nearer the truth.
- The ordering 10% / 3 far below 15% / 8 below 20% / 10 holds in every
  variant.
- Most variants move the lip moment up, so the baseline likely
  underestimates lip authority, but hinge-only refinement moves it slightly
  down and no converged value exists. For the 10% / 3 lip, whose moment is
  below 0.01, XFOIL does not constrain the value to better than a factor of
  about three.

**It is not rounding of the bend.** Three observations:

- Bend sharpness in the paneled geometry does not track the effect. The
  commanded angle is reached over 0 to 1.7% of chord and at most three
  nodes in every variant. Hinge-refined paneling is as sharp as the input
  and moves the answer by about −2%; the bluntest variant moves it +24%.
  Default re-paneling does roughly double the bend width of the 10% / 3 lip
  (0.55% to 1.35% of chord).
- The inviscid lip moment changes by at most 0.7% across the variants that
  produced viscous data.
- The clean section, which has no bend, is affected as much (below).

The behaviour is consistent with sensitivity of the laminar, separated
viscous solution to node clustering near the leading and trailing edges.
That cause was not isolated: no variant separates the two edges.

**The clean NACA 0008 moves too.** At Cl 0.25 and Re 80k its Cm runs from
−0.0125 (baseline) to −0.0114 across variants, and dCm/dCl over Cl
0.15–0.35 from −0.049 (baseline) to −0.045 (240 panels) to −0.027 (as-is).
The largest change in clean dCm/dCl across variants grows with Reynolds
number: about 16% at Re 60k, 44% at 80k and 80% at 100k. Any bent-minus-clean
difference, and the section aerodynamic-centre shifts quoted in section C,
carry an uncertainty of this order.

**Boundary-layer dumps** (baseline and as-is paneling only; Re 80k, plus
15% / 8 at 60k and 100k; `gaps/step2_separation_summary.csv`):

- Lower surface, the side the lip opens toward: laminar to the trailing
  edge in nearly every case. With a lip it separates within about 0.035
  chord of the hinge (just ahead of it for 10% / 3 at alpha 0, at or just
  aft otherwise), never reattaches, and reaches a shape factor of 8.6 to
  18.3. This is the condition named above as the one XFOIL models least
  reliably, and the dumps confirm it is present in every lip case.
- Upper surface: a laminar separation bubble that spans or sits ahead of
  the hinge at low alpha. For the large lips at Re 80k and 100k it is gone
  by the alpha that gives Cl 0.25; at Re 60k the 15% / 8 lip still has one
  there.
- Clean section: laminar separation near 0.90 chord at alpha 0 without
  reattachment, and a bubble from 0.80 to 0.91 chord at Cl 0.25.
